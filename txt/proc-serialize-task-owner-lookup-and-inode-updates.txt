Subject: proc: serialize task owner lookup and inode updates
Date: Tue, 24 Nov 2026 01:30:00 -0800

Concurrent lookups of the same /proc/<pid> entry write its inode UID and
GID without serialization.  KCSAN reports two such writes.  If the task
changes credentials between lookups, an older owner can also overwrite a
newer one.  An AppArmor rule using the cached inode owner can then deny
access incorrectly or permit access that the new owner would not allow. 
The new syzbot report demonstrates the concurrent writes, not an actual
permission failure.

Alexey Dobriyan proposed serializing the stores in 2019 [1].  The
subsequent discussion pointed out that calculating the owner before
acquiring the lock still allows an older snapshot to be stored last [2].

Take inode->i_lock before calculating and installing the owner.  Cover
the ordinary pid, fd and map_files revalidation paths, and serialize
their concurrent mode updates where present.  On PREEMPT_RT, make
pid_revalidate() retry in ref-walk instead of acquiring the sleeping
inode lock during RCU-walk.  This adds an inode lock to procfs
revalidation, and an RCU-walk retry on PREEMPT_RT.

This serializes the procfs owner writers and prevents an older procfs
lookup from committing its owner after a newer lookup.  Security hooks
and readers of inode ownership have their own synchronization rules;
this change does not make the inode owner a snapshot of a task whose
credentials are changing concurrently.
