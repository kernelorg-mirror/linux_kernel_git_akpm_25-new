Subject: mm/swap: discard unadopted large folios during swapoff
Date: Fri, 30 Oct 2026 21:53:02 +0000

swapoff() can leave incorrect reverse-map accounting and trigger kernel
warnings when it races with a fault reading a large folio into swap cache.
Phantom mappings can also prevent the folio from being freed.

Read completion unlocks the folio before the fault adopts it as anonymous
memory.  If swapoff takes the folio lock first, unuse_pte() encounters a
large !anon folio.  It calls folio_add_new_anon_rmap(), which accounts for
the whole folio, but installs only one PTE.

Like do_swap_page() when it cannot map a new large folio in full, discard
the clean swap-cache folio and leave the swap PTE unchanged.  The existing
try_to_unuse() retry loop revisits the entry, and swapoff's readahead path
allocates order-0 folios.  Racing faults revalidate swap-cache membership
under the folio lock before using the discarded folio.

Do this after validating the PTE and handling poisoned or unreadable
pages, but before changing RSS, swap counts or reverse maps.  Already
anonymous large folios retain their existing partial-mapping handling.  If
the unadopted folio is unexpectedly dirty, fail swapoff with -EBUSY rather
than discard data or initialize incorrect reverse maps.
