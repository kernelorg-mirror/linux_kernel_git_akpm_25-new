From: Andrew Morton <akpm@linux-foundation.org>
Subject: mm/rmap: avoid reusing batched TLB flush generations v2
Date: Tue, 17 Nov 2026 17:49:27 -0800

The packed mm->tlb_flush_batched counter can reuse a value while a
concurrent TLB flush is in progress. A flusher can read pending=1 and
flush the TLB, then pause before recording flushed=1. Another task can
defer 16,384 real PTE unmaps; the old 15-bit pending counter then resets
the whole packed word to 1. The first flusher's cmpxchg succeeds and
records the later unmaps as flushed, although its flush preceded them.
An mprotect or zap can then see pending=flushed=1 and skip the flush
needed before proceeding. A CPU may retain a stale writable translation
after the PTE change.

Use separate 64-bit pending and flushed counters. Record the pending
value read before the flush as flushed only after flush_tlb_mm() returns.
An unmap during the flush leaves the counts unequal, so a later check
flushes again. Concurrent flushers can write an older flushed count,
causing an extra flush, but cannot mark a newer unmap as flushed.

Fixes: be4fc7ef0f05 ("mm/rmap: fix potential batched TLB flush race")
Reported-by: Kirk Swidowski <swidowski@google.com>
Cc: <stable@vger.kernel.org>

Testing: Matched x86-64 QEMU guests used four CPUs, two NUMA nodes,
2 GiB RAM, identical kernel configurations, and the same temporary
source patch quoted below. That patch inserted task-scoped waits and
logged the real deferred PTE unmaps and TLB flushes. It did not change
the production counter updates or the decision to flush.

A one-page migration set pending=1 and waited before its own batched
flush. A one-page mprotect performed a real flush at pending=1 and
waited before recording completion. Another task migrated 20,000
populated anonymous pages. On the unpatched kernel, its 16,384th real
deferred PTE unmap reset the packed word to 1; the migration task then
waited before flushing its own batch. The earlier flusher recorded
flushed=1. A separate MADV_DONTNEED zap saw pending=flushed=1 and
skipped the TLB flush while that batch was still waiting. No real TLB
flush occurred between the earlier flush and this zap.

With the fix, those unmaps left pending=16385 and flushed=1. An at-risk
check called flush_tlb_mm() while the migration batch was still held;
the later zap saw pending=flushed=16385. Both guests migrated all
20,000 pages and the zap succeeded. The event-order check passed
on fresh A and B guest runs with the saved matching images. The test
established the missed flush. It did not observe a stale userspace
access or data corruption.
